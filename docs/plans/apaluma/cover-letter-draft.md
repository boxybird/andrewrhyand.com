Dear Apaluma Team,

I've spent the last several months building a personal version of the problem space described on your site: Groundtruth, a New Mexico water data aggregation tool that unifies groundwater levels, well permits, and water rights into a single address-searchable interface. The work has involved the kinds of problems you describe: fragmented state agency datasets, legacy records spread across file systems and library archives, decades of data that should be queryable by ordinary property owners but isn't. Your framing of "dark to light" (the way you talk about data buried in legacy systems) reads less like marketing language and more like the actual shape of the problem I've been wrestling with on my own.

I'm a senior full-stack engineer based in Albuquerque, currently shipping production work for clients at The Gecko Agency across modern stacks (Vue, Svelte, React, Astro, Laravel), and I've been a daily practitioner of agentic coding (Claude Code, Codex) since late 2025. What's changed for me over those months isn't *what* I think about as an engineer (architecture, performance, the business domain, the actual humans using the software), but how much time I have to think about it well, instead of context-switching between strategy and line-by-line typing. You note in the listing that AI tools are part of how everyone on the team works; that's been true for me too, and I've developed working judgment for what to delegate to agents, where they drift, and where my own attention has to stay.

A senior engineering role at Apaluma lines up unusually well for me: mission, technology, and geography all in one place. The end users you describe (regulators, environmental scientists, program managers who aren't power users) match the kind of people I've spent five years building interfaces for in an agency context, where the bar is whether an actual person can find what they came for, fast. I'd welcome the chance to walk through the architecture decisions in Groundtruth and Movie Madness with the team, since they overlap meaningfully with what Apaluma is doing.

Thank you for reading.

Andrew Rhyand
andrew.rhyand@gmail.com · (503) 505-8485 · andrewrhyand.com
